## TL;DR

- **Video**: *The biggest AI breakthrough in medicine & drug discovery*, channel **AI Search (@theAIsearch)**, uploaded **2026-05-13**, length **31:54 (1,914 s)**, 122,790 views at retrieval; the description links **https://www.nature.com/articles/s44386-026-00047-4** [1]. ✅ So the new paper the video refers to is the **npj Drug Discovery** version published 2026-05-04 [2], nine days before upload — a journal publication of the October 2024 arXiv preprint whose benchmark table is unchanged since v1 [3].
- Of **77 substantive claims** extracted (the numbered table below has 77 rows): **33 accurate**, **19 mostly accurate** (true but imprecise or missing a material caveat), **10 exaggerated**, **8 misleading**, **4 inaccurate**, **2 unverifiable**, **1 ASR-split** (#28, where the two transcripts differ). [verifier-corrected: the draft said "61 substantive claims … 31 / 13 / 8 / 6 / 2 / 1", which matched neither the 77-row table nor the draft's own tally block; the counts above are a row-by-row recount, and they include the two severity downgrades noted in the Verification notes.]
- **The single worst error** is repeated twice: the claim that MAMMAL understands DNA and could take a patient's DNA sample as input. MAMMAL has **no nucleotide modality at all** — its four sub-tokenizers are amino acids, SMILES, ranked gene *symbols*, and cell/tissue ontology terms; the shipped vocabulary contains no DNA sequence tokenizer and no DNA entity token [5]. ✅
- **The second worst** is the framing of the carfilzomib result as overturning a settled expert belief. The paper says carfilzomib has "**limited efficacy in cells of solid tumors**" and that the result "**warrants further investigation**" [2]; published 2014 work already reported carfilzomib IC50s of "< 1.0 nM to 36 nM" in five NSCLC cell lines [8], with the recognised obstacle being solubility/instability, i.e. delivery, not target-cell insensitivity [9]. ✅ **But this is an exaggeration of a real clinical limitation, not an invention** [verifier-adjusted]: carfilzomib has been approved only for multiple myeloma since July 2012, and proteasome inhibitors as a class have never converted in-vitro potency into a solid-tumour indication, so "it doesn't work in solid tumours" is defensible *about clinical outcomes*. The video's two specific errors are turning that into a categorical belief, attributed to expert oncologists, that the drug is useless, and presenting cell-line potency as new knowledge when it was published a decade earlier.
- **The third** is the AlphaFold 3 comparison: the video presents a symmetric head-to-head. In reality MAMMAL was **fine-tuned on binders/non-binders of those very targets** while AF3 was used **zero-shot**, scored via ipTM confidence as a binding proxy, on **28–60 samples per target**; the paper calls it "**an exploratory comparison**" [2]. Un-fine-tuned MAMMAL scores AUROC **0.530** on HER2 — chance [2, Suppl. Table S5]. ✅
- **Settling the transcript ambiguity at ~12:29**: the auto-captions are right. BBBP 0.937 → **0.957** (+2.0 points, +2.2 % relative); ClinTox 0.948 → **0.986** (+3.8 points, +4.0 % relative) [2, Table 1]. "**A few**" percentage points is correct; Whisper's "a huge" is both an ASR artefact and an overstatement. ✅
- Verdicts that matter most for the dossier's own use of MAMMAL: **SOTA on all 11 benchmarks: false (9 of 11)**; **95 % of 805 cell lines: overstates the paper's "approximately 90–95 %"**; **19 % improvement on CDR-H3: relative, and CDR-L3 actually improved more (+39 %)**; **7.5 % on Zheng68k: correct** (relative F1); **2 billion pre-training samples: correct as the paper states it**; **beat AF3 on 5 of 7: correct**.
- Overall: the **biology and ML explanations are unusually good for the genre**, and no benchmark number is fabricated. The **hype is in the framing, not the figures** — superlatives, a missing-caveat AF3 comparison, an **overstated** experts-were-wrong-for-decades narrative [verifier-adjusted from "fabricated": see the carfilzomib bullet above — the video inflates a genuine clinical limitation rather than inventing one], and a capability (DNA) the model does not have. **Hype level: 7/10.**

## Method

## Overall assessment

**Accuracy of the technical content: good.** Every benchmark number the video states is traceable to the paper, and I found **no fabricated figures**. The explanations of SMILES, ranked gene-expression input, the modular tokenizer, the shared embedding space, CDRs and the sequence-only constraint on antibody design are all correct and better than typical science-YouTube fare. The claims of 5 of 7 targets, 7.5 % on Zheng68k, 19 % on CDR-H3, 805 cell lines, 2 billion samples, 3 of 4 drugs below Tanimoto 0.7, the Nobel Prize, 90 % drug failure and ~$1 B per drug all check out — in several cases because the video is faithfully repeating the paper's own phrasing.

**Accuracy of the framing: poor in four specific places.** The video (i) upgrades 9-of-11 to 11-of-11; (ii) **inflates a genuine clinical limitation into** a decades-long expert consensus that carfilzomib cannot touch solid-tumour cells — the drug does lack any solid-tumour approval, but the contrary *preclinical* data have been published since 2014, the paper itself claims only "limited efficacy", and the decades timeframe misstates a 14-year timeline [verifier-adjusted from "invents"]; (iii) presents the AlphaFold 3 comparison as a symmetric contest, omitting that MAMMAL was fine-tuned on those exact targets while AF3 was zero-shot and scored through a confidence proxy on 28–60 samples, and omitting that un-fine-tuned MAMMAL is at chance; and (iv) attributes to MAMMAL a DNA-reading capability it does not possess, then builds a personalised-medicine vision on top of it.

**A pattern worth naming:** the errors consistently run in the direction of **attributing results to emergent understanding rather than to supervised fine-tuning**. Claims that the model truly understands biology's rules, that it relies on the mathematics of the sequence, and that the AI confidently declared its verdict — whereas Supplementary Tables S5–S6 show the foundation model alone cannot do the antibody task at all, and Table 1's footnote states every result comes from a separately fine-tuned model. The genuinely interesting scientific story (a 458 M-parameter T5 with a clever shared tokenizer is a *better starting point* for 11 different fine-tuning tasks than task-specific architectures) is less dramatic than the story told, but it is the one the paper supports.

**Hype level: 7/10.** Not fabrication; systematic inflation. A viewer would come away believing that an AI with genome-reading ability has already overturned oncological consensus, beaten AlphaFold at its own job, and put cancer cures months away — none of which the paper claims. A viewer would also come away correctly informed about what a modular multi-modal tokenizer is and why it might help, which is more than most coverage achieves.

**For the dossier:** treat the video as a *navigation aid* to the paper, never as a source. Every number we reuse downstream should be taken from `mammal_paper_deep_dive.md`, which cites the journal tables directly.
