## TL;DR

- **Nothing is approved.** As of 2026-10-04 there is **no FDA-approved and no FDA-conditionally-approved drug for lifespan or healthspan extension in dogs** (or any species). Nothing has launched. ✅ [1][2]
- **Loyal (Cellular Longevity, Inc.)** is furthest along. Its lead program **LOY-002** (daily pill, dogs ≥10 yr and ≥14 lb, mechanism = improving insulin sensitivity / lowering insulin, positioned as a caloric-restriction mimetic) has FDA CVM acceptance of **two of three** technical sections required for Expanded Conditional Approval (XCA): **RXE accepted Feb 2025** ✅ [3][4] and **Target Animal Safety accepted 13 Jan 2026** ✅ [5]. The remaining section is **Chemistry, Manufacturing & Controls (CMC)**, listed as "in progress" on Loyal's own site ✅ [1][2]; the company said in Jan 2026 it expected to complete the final section and apply for XCA "next year" 🟡 [5] — **in a 13 Jan 2026 release "next year" literally reads as 2027; Pet Age's paraphrase does not resolve which year is meant, and Loyal's site gives no date** [verifier-corrected].
- All **three** Loyal candidates now have RXE acceptances: LOY-001 (injectable, large dogs, Nov 2023) ✅ [6][7], LOY-002 (Feb 2025) ✅ [3], **LOY-003 (daily pill for large/giant dogs, RXE completed 16 Sep 2026 — the most recent Loyal milestone I could find)** ✅ [8][9].
- Loyal's pivotal **STAY** study finished enrolling **1,300 dogs at 72 US clinics in July 2025** ✅ [10]; ~4-year duration, randomised placebo-controlled, first dog dosed Dec 2023 🟡 [3]. Results are years away.
- **TRIAD** (Dog Aging Project's rapamycin trial) is the only academic lifespan RCT. Published protocol: double-masked, randomised, placebo-controlled, multicentre; **target n = 580 (290/290)**; dogs **≥7 yr and 20–55 kg inclusive** in the protocol (recruitment pages say "≥44 lb") [verifier-corrected]; **oral rapamycin 0.15 mg/kg once weekly for 1 year + 2-year observation**; primary endpoint **survival time from randomisation (intention-to-treat)** ✅ [11][12] [verifier-corrected]. **No results published as of Oct 2026.** Enrollment was **170 dogs at 20 sites in Dec 2024** ✅ [14]; a later dog-owner blog (Urban Dog NYC, dated 30 Jan, year not shown — context implies 2026) says **235 enrolled, >42 completed, enrollment hoped to finish end-2026, study concluding ~2029** 🟡 [66] [verifier-corrected: this figure is **not** in the BBC Science Focus article previously cited].
- The strongest *actual* evidence that dog ageing is malleable remains **1990s–2000s nutrition work**: Kealy et al. 2002 showed 25% lifetime food restriction significantly extended median lifespan in 48 paired Labradors and delayed chronic-disease onset ✅ [16] (**13.0 vs 11.2 yr, P < 0.01**, +1.8 yr / +16% — confirmed in the JAVMA full text ✅ [16] [verifier-corrected]).
- **Gene therapy in dogs is technically proven but not for ageing.** Dogs were the pivotal large-animal model for RPE65 retinal gene therapy (→ Luxturna) 🟡 [18], haemophilia B AAV 🟡 [19], and AAV-CRISPR dystrophin restoration (Amoasii 2018: 3–90% of normal in skeletal muscle, 92% in cardiac at the highest dose, 8 weeks after systemic delivery, n=2) ✅ [20]. The only ageing-adjacent dog gene therapy with real data is **Rejuvenate Bio's RJB-01** (AAV; FGF21 up / TGFβ1 down via sTGFβR2) in Cavalier King Charles Spaniels with mitral valve disease — **conference-abstract-level pilot data only, no p-values, n=17** 🟡 [21].
- The headline anti-ageing gene-therapy result (Davidsohn 2019 PNAS: one AAV formulation treating four age-related diseases) is **mouse-only** ✅ [22], as is Rejuvenate Bio's OSK partial-reprogramming "+109% remaining lifespan" result ✅ [23] (peer-reviewed abstract, *Cell Reprogram* 2024) [verifier-corrected].
- The commercial supplement space (e.g. **Leap Years**, Animal Bioscience) rests on **one 70-dog RCT** whose only significant finding was an owner-rated cognition scale at 3 months (p=0.02); every objective measure — activity, gait speed, in-clinic cognitive tasks, frailty — was non-significant, and 60% of placebo dogs "improved" ✅ [24].

### 1.2 What the RXE acceptances actually mean

RXE ("reasonable expectation of effectiveness") is **one technical section** of a conditional-approval application, not an approval and not a finding that the drug works. Loyal's own description: XCA requires **full TAS + full CMC + RXE** ✅ [27]. The 2023 LOY-001 announcement is nevertheless a genuine first: FDA CVM accepted that **lifespan extension can be a legitimate drug indication** ✅ [6][7].

LOY-001's RXE package reportedly comprised: interventional studies in "an FDA-accepted model of canine aging", an **observational study of 451 dogs** with no-drug control, biomarker endpoints, owner-measured quality-of-life scores and vet-measured health outcomes; the technical section exceeded **2,300 pages** ✅ [6]. (dvm360 described the observational component as **452 companion dogs across 84 breeds, ages 2–18** 🟡 [7]; Loyal's own peer-reviewed cross-sectional paper enrolled **451** dogs ✅ [28] — the 451/452 discrepancy is unresolved ❓.)

LOY-002's published scientific underpinning is Loyal's own 2025 *Scientific Reports* paper (all 18 authors at Cellular Longevity Inc.): in 451 mature adult companion dogs, **elevated ALP, ALT, cholesterol, insulin, free fatty acids, palmitic and oleic acid correlated with higher frailty scores**, while higher glucose and adiponectin correlated with *lower* frailty; higher ALP, ALT and insulin associated with lower health-related quality of life. The authors explicitly state it is **cross-sectional** and that "future research should investigate the causality of these associations" ✅ [28].

### 1.3 LOY-002 safety package (accepted 13 Jan 2026)

- A standard target-animal-safety study at **1×, 3× and 5× dose**, with no clinically significant adverse events ✅ [1][64]
- Plus **field safety data from >400 dogs** receiving LOY-002 inside the STAY study, "for varying durations, including for up to one year" ✅ [1][5][64] [verifier-corrected: duration added]
- FDA agreed the data support safety for the proposed conditions of use ✅ [5]

### 1.6 Criticisms

**Scientific / evidentiary**
- **LOY-002's molecular identity has never been disclosed.** Independent commentators note it is said not to be rapamycin, metformin, a somatostatin analogue or a GH/IGF-1 axis drug, and speculate about PPAR agonism — explicitly speculation 🟡 [26].
- The IGF-1 rationale for LOY-001/LOY-003 has been attacked as correlational: small dogs have lower IGF-1 *and* live longer, which does not establish that lowering IGF-1 lengthens life; published IGF-1 values vary enormously *within* a weight class, and the blogger reports only a weak correlation (r≈0.07) between IGF-1 and age in an 88-dog 2011 dataset (the post frames it against age, not clearly against age at death) 🟡 [30] [verifier-corrected]. **This critique is a non-peer-reviewed blog post (Dec 2023) and should be weighted accordingly**, but Loyal's own peer-reviewed data are also explicitly cross-sectional ✅ [28].
- Veterinary oncologist Daniela Korec: the pilot evidence base was small ("only 48 dogs over the course of three months"), the full composition is undisclosed, and long-term inhibition of a normal physiological function carries unknown risk 🟡 [31]. (Note: the "48 dogs / 3 months" figure is Korec's characterisation as reported by Slate; I could not verify it against a Loyal study document ❓.)

**Ethical**
- Bioethicist Jessica Pierce argues the drugs serve owners rather than dogs — "These drugs are not about dogs or dog welfare. They're about humans" — and that dogs cannot consent, so owner decisions "may have a profound capacity to inflict suffering", including keeping animals alive past the humane point 🟡 [31].
- Korec separately warns against conflating quantity of life with quality 🟡 [31].

**Commercial / access**
- Halioua said in 2023 that pricing would be "in the double digits per month for pet parents" 🟡 [7].
- Veterinary-market commentary (Linda Rhodes, VMD, PhD) has been broadly favourable, framing lifespan extension as "a new indication" 🟡 [3][7].

### 2.1 TRIAD — Test of Rapamycin In Aging Dogs

Primary protocol paper: **Coleman AE et al., GeroScience 47(3):2851–2877 (2025)**, DOI 10.1007/s11357-024-01484-7 ✅ [11].

| Attribute | Value | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| Design | Prospective, parallel-group, **double-masked**, randomised, **placebo-controlled**, multicentre | ✅ [11] |
| Self-description | "the first rigorous test of a pharmacologic intervention against biological aging with lifespan and healthspan metrics as endpoints to be performed outside of the laboratory in any species" | ✅ [11] |
| Target n | **580 dogs (290 rapamycin / 290 placebo)**; >80% power to detect a **6% difference in mean total lifespan** | ✅ [11][14] [verifier-corrected: allocation and power added from protocol] |

Kate Creevy's framing of the mechanism: rapamycin "works by modifying the cells' energy balance and energy handling… It seems to mimic the effects that happen in people or animals who do intermittent fasting" ✅ [14]. Matt Kaeberlein has publicly put it at "better than 50/50" that rapamycin increases lifespan and improves health in dogs, with a possible ">20% increase in healthy lifespan" in larger dogs 🟡 [15].

### 2.2 Earlier rapamycin pilots in dogs

| Study | Design | Result | Tag |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Urfer et al. 2017**, GeroScience 39(2):117–127, DOI 10.1007/s11357-017-9972-z | **24 middle-aged healthy companion dogs** (23 completed: placebo 8 / 0.05 mg/kg 5 / 0.1 mg/kg 10, each dose given 3× weekly), **10 weeks**, echo before/after [verifier-corrected: doses added] | **No clinical side effects**; echo "suggested improvement" in **E/A ratio, fractional shortening and ejection fraction**; MCV decreased but haematology stayed in range; authors call for larger, longer trials | ✅ [33] |

**Honest read:** the cardiac "benefit" headline rests on a 10-week, n=24 study (only fractional shortening was significant on both statistical tests; EF was not) whose follow-up at a **2–4× lower weekly dose** (0.075 vs 0.15–0.3 mg/kg/week) found no echocardiographic difference — though with n=17 it was small [verifier-corrected: "6×" was wrong]. The dog rapamycin evidence base today is a **safety and tolerability** story, not an efficacy story.

### 2.3 DAP observational findings relevant to interventions

- **Once-daily feeding — Bray et al. 2022**, GeroScience 44(3):1779–1790, DOI 10.1007/s11357-022-00575-7. Cross-sectional; **n = 10,474** for cognition and **n = 24,238** for health conditions. Controlling for sex, age, breed and other confounders, dogs fed **once daily** had **lower mean cognitive-dysfunction scores** and **lower odds of gastrointestinal, dental, orthopedic, kidney/urinary and liver/pancreas disorders**. The authors are explicit that longitudinal data are needed for causal inference ✅ [36]. **Reverse causation is a live concern** (sick dogs may be fed more often) and the paper does not resolve it ✅.
- Review-level DAP outputs: a microbiome ageing "clock" built on **922 dogs** and metabolomic ageing signatures in **784 dogs** 🟡 [37].
- A biological-age clock from routine clinical markers predicting health trajectory and mortality exists — built by **Nestlé Research** authors on 829 dogs followed >12 years, **not** a DAP product (Herzig et al., GeroScience 47(1):45–59, 2025) ✅ [38] [verifier-corrected] — relevant as a potential **surrogate endpoint** for future trials.

## 3. Caloric restriction — the best-established intervention in dogs

**Kealy RD, Lawler DF, Ballam JM, Mantz SL, Biery DN, Greeley EH, Lust G, Segre M, Smith GK, Stowe HD. "Effects of diet restriction on life span and age-related changes in dogs." JAVMA 220(9):1315–1320 (2002).**

Confirmed from the abstract and full text ✅ [16]:
- **48 Labrador Retrievers**, paired from **8 weeks of age**, one of each pair fed **25% less** than its partner **until death**
- Annual serum biochemistry, body-condition scoring and body-composition measurement through age 12
- Food-restricted dogs had **lower body weight, lower body fat**, and **lower serum triglycerides, triiodothyronine, insulin and glucose**
- **"Median life span was significantly longer for dogs in which food was restricted"**
- **Onset of signs of chronic disease was generally delayed**

Numbers from the JAVMA full text (Results section), verified by Opus 5.5 [verifier-corrected]:
- Median lifespan **13.0 yr (restricted) vs 11.2 yr (control)**, **P < 0.01** = **+1.8 yr / +16%** ✅ [16]
- Maximum lifespan (age at 90% mortality) **14.0 vs 12.9 yr — not significant** ✅ [16]
- Age by which 50% of dogs still had not needed long-term **osteoarthritis treatment**: **13.3 yr (restricted) vs 10.3 yr (control)**, P < 0.01; for **any chronic condition**: **12.0 vs 9.9 yr**, P = 0.016 ✅ [16]
- Loyal's blog says arthritis developed "1.5 years later on average" 🟡 [17] — **this figure does not appear in the 2002 JAVMA paper**, whose own OA-treatment measure differs by ~3 yr [verifier-corrected]
- Loyal's blog says mean age at death from cancer was **11.6 vs 9.7 yr** 🟡 [17] — **not in the 2002 JAVMA paper**; origin (possibly a later Lawler et al. paper) not confirmed ❓
- The 2008 *British Journal of Nutrition* review of the same cohort (Lawler et al., 99(4):793–805) reports a **"1.8 years longer median lifespan"** — consistent with 13.0 vs 11.2 — with delayed late-life disease, especially osteoarthritis, and high fat mass / insulin resistance predicting earlier death ✅ [39]. **The earlier "13.2 vs 11.0 yr" attribution to this paper is not supported by its abstract and has been removed** [verifier-corrected].

Follow-up literature exists on physiological variables under lifetime restriction in the same colony ❓ [40] (citation located, full text not opened).

**Why this matters for intervention design:** this is a **lifetime, littermate-paired, single-breed, laboratory-housed** study. It establishes that dog lifespan is plastic; it does **not** tell you that a pill mimicking some of CR's metabolic signature reproduces the effect. Loyal's blog presents this study as foundational to CR-mimetic drug development (without naming LOY-002) while stating it has no affiliation with Purina ✅ [17] [verifier-corrected: the post does not explicitly link the study to LOY-002].

## 4. Gene therapy and gene editing (interventional, somatic)

### 4.1 Rejuvenate Bio — the only ageing-adjacent dog gene therapy with clinical data

**RJB-01**, for myxomatous mitral valve disease (MMVD) in Cavalier King Charles Spaniels. AAV vector; **upregulates FGF21 and downregulates TGFβ1 (delivered as sTGFβR2)** 🟡 [21].

Data presented at **ASGCT, 18 May 2023** (conference abstract; **no peer-reviewed publication found**) 🟡 [21]:
- **n = 17 dogs total; n = 12 received RJB-01 + pimobendan**
- Durable expression up to **3 years**; **4 dogs** monitored >2 years
- Disease progression delayed **>1.5 years** vs standard of care; ~**600-day** delay vs pimobendan alone, ~**800 days** vs placebo
- Left atrial size **reduced by 0.3 units over 28 months**, vs a **0.8-unit increase** on pimobendan alone; some dogs reverted to an earlier disease stage
- **No p-values reported**; favourable safety profile claimed
- Noah Davidsohn (Rejuvenate Bio): "Mitral valve disease is often fatal for canines at the advanced stage, with no current treatments that can provide a cure." 🟡 [21]

**Evidence caveats:** small n, non-randomised comparison to historical/standard-of-care arms, no inferential statistics published, abstract-only. Treat as **pilot data**.

Corporate status:
- **Phibro Animal Health** partnership announced **22–23 June 2022**, with intent to file for conditional approval "as early as 2023" 🟡 [41]. **I found no evidence that a conditional-approval application was filed or granted** ❓ — Rejuvenate Bio's "In the News" page has no 2025–2026 entries ✅ [42], and its press-release page (newest item 8 Jun 2026) mentions no FDA filing or conditional approval ✅ [65] [verifier-corrected].
- **8 June 2026 (San Diego dateline): $6M financing led by VCapital with Merck Animal Health, Kendall Capital Partners, Connecticut Innovations and Digitalis participating, plus a strategic R&D collaboration with Merck Animal Health** "advancing a targeted gene therapy program"; the proceeds support the pipeline "including treatments designed to address chronic and age-associated diseases in companion animals" ✅ [65] [verifier-corrected: confirmed from the company release; the release itself does not name RJB-01, RJB-0402 or FGF21]. A second program, **RJB-0402**, is a liver-directed **FGF21** therapy 🟡 [43]; an earlier RJB-0402 preclinical readout was in arrhythmogenic cardiomyopathy 🟡 [44].

### 4.2 The mouse evidence Rejuvenate Bio is built on (clearly mouse-only)

**Davidsohn N, Pezone M, Vernet A, Graveline A, Oliver D, Slomovic S, Punthambaker S, Sun X, Liao R, Bonventre JV, Church GM. "A single combination gene therapy treats multiple age-related diseases." PNAS 116(47):23505–23511 (2019).** ✅ [22]

AAV-delivered **FGF21**, **αKlotho**, and **soluble TGFβ receptor 2 (sTGFβR2)**, singly and in combination, across four mouse disease models:
- **+58% heart function** in ascending-aortic-constriction heart failure
- **−38% α-smooth muscle actin** expression
- **−75% renal medullary atrophy** in unilateral ureteral obstruction
- **Complete reversal** of obesity and diabetes phenotypes on constant high-fat diet
- **One formulation combining two of the therapies treated all four diseases**

**No dog data in this paper. No dog ageing program from this combination has been reported.** ✅

**Partial reprogramming (OSK).** Rejuvenate Bio reported AAV9-delivered doxycycline-inducible **Oct4/Sox2/Klf4**, cyclically induced in **124-week-old** male wild-type mice (~77 human years), giving a **109% increase in median *remaining* lifespan** ✅ [23] (treated +18.5 weeks vs control +8.86 weeks 🟡 — figures from the company release), with improved frailty scores ✅; the epigenetic age-reversal markers were shown in **human keratinocytes** expressing OSK, not in the mice ✅ [23] [verifier-corrected]. Published as Macip CC et al., *Cellular Reprogramming* 26(1):24–32 (2024), DOI 10.1089/cell.2023.0072, after a Jan 2023 bioRxiv preprint ✅ [23][45]. **Mouse only; no canine OSK study found** ❓. Life Biosciences is running a parallel OSK ("partial epigenetic reprogramming") platform in liver and ocular disease, presented at ARDD 2025 🟡 [46] — again no dog work.

### 4.3 Dogs as the proving ground for in vivo gene delivery (not ageing, but feasibility)

| System | Key result | Tag |
|---|---|---|
| **RPE65 / Briard dogs** | Acland et al. 2001, *Nature Genetics* 28:92–95 — subretinal AAV-RPE65 restored vision in a naturally occurring canine model of Leber congenital amaurosis; the pivotal large-animal step toward **voretigene neparvovec (Luxturna)** 🟡 [18]. Primary follow-up (Acland et al., *Mol Ther* 12:1072–1082, 2005) reports ERG responses stable over **3 years**; the ">7 years" (e.g. "Lancelot") durability claim could not be confirmed in a primary source ❓ [verifier-corrected] | 🟡 [18] |
| **Haemophilia B dogs** | Liver-directed AAV factor IX (1×10¹² vg/kg) gave sustained (>17 months) FIX at 5–12% of normal and near-complete phenotypic correction in 3 of 4 dogs, including 2 with FIX-null mutations; the 4th developed an inhibitor (Mount et al., *Blood* 99(8):2670–2676, 2002). AAV2/8 re-dosing work followed (not re-checked) | ✅ [19] [verifier-corrected: upgraded after reading abstract] |
| **AAV-CRISPR in ΔE50-MD dogs** | **Amoasii et al. 2018, Science 362(6410):86–91**, DOI 10.1126/science.aau1549. AAV delivered Cas9 + guide to skip exon 51 in **4 dogs**: 6 weeks after intramuscular (n=2) or **8 weeks after systemic (n=2)** delivery, dystrophin reached **3–90% of normal in skeletal muscle** and **92% of normal in cardiac muscle in the highest-dose dog**, with improved histology. Authors frame it as proof-of-concept needing "further development" | ✅ [20] |
| **LPL, MPS and other canine models** | Widely cited as AAV/lentiviral proof-of-concept species | ❓ (not individually verified in this pass) |
| **GRMD + NAD precursor** | Cardoso et al. 2023 (*Skeletal Muscle* 13:20): GRMD dogs have reduced striated-muscle NAD⁺; nicotinamide supplementation "modestly reduces aspects of striated muscle disease" — the earlier "improved muscle function" wording is not supported by the abstract | ✅ [47] [verifier-corrected: primary citation located, effect size softened] |

**What dogs prove:** systemic AAV delivery, long-term transgene expression, and in vivo CRISPR editing all **work in a ~20–40 kg outbred carnivore**. That is the single most important technical enabler for any future interventional dog-ageing gene therapy. What they do **not** prove: durability over a dog lifetime, immunological safety of re-dosing, or anything about ageing endpoints.

### 5.1 The ITP as the pipeline for what to try in dogs

The NIA Interventions Testing Program is the cleanest ranked list of pharmacological lifespan effects in a mammal. Headline median-lifespan effects, as tabulated in the 2025 ITP sex-differences review ✅ [56] [verifier-corrected: rapamycin rows replaced; the earlier rows were the 20-month late-onset cohort]:

| Compound | Female | Male |
|---|---|---|
| Rapamycin 14 ppm (from 9 mo; two cohorts) | +17.5% / +22.5% | +7.5% / +9.5% |
| Rapamycin 42 ppm (from 9 mo) | +28.3% | +21.9% |
| Rapamycin 14 ppm (late onset, from 20 mo) | +13.4% | +19.5% |
| Rapamycin 42 ppm (late onset, from 20 mo) | +15.0% | +10.9% |
| Acarbose 1000 ppm (from 8 mo) | +4.8% | +17.5% |
| 17α-estradiol 14.4 ppm (from 10 mo) | +2% (NS) | +17% |
| Canagliflozin 180 ppm (from 7 mo) | +1% (NS) | +14% |

**Two strategic lessons for dog work.** (1) The four largest ITP effects — canagliflozin, acarbose, 17α-estradiol, rapamycin — are all plausible dog candidates, and only rapamycin is being tested in dogs. (2) **Sex-specificity is the rule, not the exception** in the ITP corpus 🟡 [56]; a dog trial powered on a pooled estimate could miss or dilute a real male-only effect. Note also that most ITP dogs-relevant translation is untested: the ITP uses genetically heterogeneous UM-HET3 mice, not an outbred species with 40-fold body-size variation.

## 6. Regulatory landscape

### 6.1 Expanded conditional approval (the pathway Loyal is using)

- **Legal basis:** ADUFA IV, signed **August 2018**, amended **section 571 of the FD&C Act** to extend conditional approval beyond MUMS (minor use / minor species) to **certain major uses in major species** ✅ [59].
- **Two eligibility criteria** under §571(a)(1)(A)(ii): the drug must (1) treat a **serious or life-threatening disease or condition, or address an unmet animal or human health need**, **and** (2) **demonstration of effectiveness would require a complex or particularly difficult study or studies** ✅ [59].
- **Guidance:** **CVM GFI #261, "Eligibility Criteria for Expanded Conditional Approval of New Animal Drugs," final, July 2021** ✅ [59].
- **Standard:** the sponsor must show the drug is **safe** and **properly manufactured** to full approval standards, but only a **reasonable expectation of effectiveness** rather than substantial evidence ✅ [59].
- **Duration:** marketable for **up to five years** while the effectiveness study completes ✅ [27][59]. Statute: a conditional approval "is effective for a 1-year period and is thereafter renewable … for up to 4 additional 1-year terms" (21 U.S.C. §360ccc(d)) ✅ [67] [verifier-corrected: confirmed in the statute].
- **The three technical sections** a sponsor must get accepted: **RXE + full TAS + full CMC** ✅ [27].
- **Failure consequence:** if the completed effectiveness study does not demonstrate full efficacy, the product comes off the market ✅ [27].

**How "lifespan extension" is being evaluated.** Loyal's argument — accepted by FDA CVM at least to the RXE stage — is that demonstrating lifespan extension is intrinsically a "complex or particularly difficult study": you must dose dogs early and follow them for the rest of their lives, which Loyal operationalised as **1,000+ dogs across 55–72 clinics over ~4 years** ✅ [27][10]. CVM has issued "concurrence" that a longevity drug *could* be approved if the pre-agreed endpoints are met ✅ [10]. **The agency has not published a general framework or guidance document for lifespan-extension claims** that I could find ❓ — the standard is currently being set case-by-case through Loyal's application.

### 6.2 Intentional genomic alterations (IGAs) in animals

- Heritable IGAs in animals are regulated by FDA CVM as **new animal drugs** 🟡 [60].
- **GFI #187A, "Heritable Intentional Genomic Alterations in Animals: Risk-Based Approach"** — **final, May 2024** ✅ [61] (read in the FDA PDF) [verifier-corrected]. It sorts products into three categories: **Category 1** (no application or prior review — limited to non-food species regulated by another agency, e.g. APHIS-regulated insects, and non-food species kept in contained laboratory research, e.g. mice and rats), **Category 2** (no application expected after FDA reviews risk-factor data; written response targeted within 180 days), **Category 3** (FDA review and approval required, with data requirements proportionate to risk) ✅ [61].
- **GFI #187B, "Heritable Intentional Genomic Alterations in Animals: The Approval Process"** — draft notice **2 May 2024**, **final January 2025** 🟡 [60]. It addresses which IGAs can go in one application (all must be present in the commercialised animal and contribute to the same intended use), required molecular and phenotypic characterisation, durability across generations, food-safety evaluation where relevant, and environmental assessment including containment 🟡 [60].
- **GFI #251 (draft, January 2025)** covers IGAs in food-producing species used as disease models in contained research settings; comments closed 10 March 2025 🟡 [60].
- **Companion-animal applicability is the key open question.** The 187A PDF itself never mentions dogs or companion animals; its examples centre on **food-producing species and research models** ✅ [61] [verifier-corrected: checked in primary]. The nearest pet precedent it cites is **GloFish** (ornamental fluorescent aquarium fish), marketed without an approval application under enforcement discretion ✅ [61] — a non-food pet, but not a mammal or a companion dog. A gene-edited pet dog would still fall under the new-animal-drug framework in principle, but **no precedent approval, category determination or guidance example for a companion dog exists that I could locate** ❓.
- **Non-heritable somatic gene therapy in dogs** (RJB-01, AAV-CRISPR) is regulated as a conventional new animal drug / biologic, not under 187A/B — which is why Rejuvenate Bio talks about conditional approval rather than IGA categories ✅ (inferred from [41][21], not from an FDA document ❓).

### 6.3 EU / UK

- EU veterinary medicines are governed by **Regulation (EU) 2019/6** via EMA; the UK by the **Veterinary Medicines Regulations** administered by the **VMD** 🟡 [62].
- **I found no evidence of any EMA or VMD position, guidance, or application concerning lifespan-extension claims in dogs, and no indication that Loyal has filed in Europe** ❓. Neither jurisdiction has an obvious analogue of US expanded conditional approval for this use case, and this is a genuine unexplored gap.

## Open questions / gaps

1. **What is LOY-002?** The molecule is undisclosed. Without it, no independent assessment of mechanism, class effects, drug interactions or long-term risk is possible. This is the field's largest single information deficit ❓.
2. **STAY's primary endpoint.** For the largest trial in veterinary history, the pre-specified primary endpoint, its statistical definition, powering assumptions, and interim-analysis plan are not in any public document I could open ❓. A verifier should check whether a protocol or synopsis has been deposited anywhere (AVMA AAHSD, a journal, or an FDA FOI summary once/if XCA is granted).
3. **Has any Loyal CMC acceptance occurred since 16 Sep 2026?** Loyal's blog's newest post as of today is the LOY-003 RXE (16 Sep 2026) ✅ [63]. I could not rule out an announcement in the last two weeks via other channels ❓.
4. **LOY-001 and LOY-003 safety/CMC status** is simply not public — neither TAS nor CMC acceptance has been announced for either ❓.
5. **TRIAD's exact current enrollment and whether any interim/safety analysis exists.** 170 (Dec 2024) is confirmed ✅; the 235 figure comes from a dog-owner blog [66] (not BBC Science Focus) whose year is implied, not stated ❓ [verifier-corrected].
6. ~~Kealy 2002's exact median lifespans~~ **Resolved by verifier:** JAVMA full text gives 13.0 vs 11.2 yr (P < 0.01); the 2008 BJN review says "1.8 years longer", consistent; the "13.2 vs 11.0" attribution was unsupported and removed [verifier-corrected]. Still open: the source of Loyal's "cancer deaths 11.6 vs 9.7 yr" and "arthritis 1.5 yr later" figures ❓.
7. **RJB-01 regulatory reality.** Whether a conditional-approval application was ever filed with FDA CVM is unknown; no primary document found ❓. The June 2026 Merck collaboration is now confirmed from the company's own Business Wire release ✅ [65] [verifier-corrected].
8. **Did any 451 vs 452 dog discrepancy** between Loyal's RXE package description and its published paper matter? Probably trivial, but unresolved ❓.
9. **No dog data at all** for: spermidine ✅ (searched and confirmed absent), acarbose, 17α-estradiol, canagliflozin, plasma factors, partial reprogramming, senolytics as monotherapy ❓. These are the obvious "next trials" and nobody is running them.
10. **No FDA framework document for lifespan claims.** The standard is being set by a single company's application, with no public guidance and no advisory-committee record I could find ❓.
11. **EU/UK**: entirely unexplored ❓.
12. **Surrogate endpoints.** Several canine biological-age clocks exist (clinical-marker, microbiome, metabolomic, methylation) 🟡 [37][38], but none is validated as a regulatory surrogate. This is a tractable, high-leverage research path.
